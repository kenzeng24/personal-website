SELECTED RESEARCH

Much of the existing work in AI for cancer focuses on initial diagnosis, with less attention paid to what happens afterward. My work at Ataraxis AI uses machine learning to explore patient outcomes after diagnosis. Currently, I focus on two related questions in breast cancer: how likely is the cancer to recur, and who may benefit from chemotherapy?

Breast cancer prognosis (ATX RISK)

I trained, validated, and deployed a model that estimates recurrence risk from H&E pathology slides and clinical data to stratify patients by risk. The ensemble is trained using a discrete-time loss to predict the probability of cancer recurrence within five years.

Chemotherapy benefit prediction · CTX

I trained, validated, and deployed models to estimate individualized chemotherapy benefit from observational cohorts using techniques from causal inference.

RESULTS & VALIDATION

The breast cancer prognostication work includes validation across five external cohorts.

HOW IT WORKS

From H&E histology to patient-level prognosis

Our model processes gigapixel H&E slides — extracting patches, encoding morphological features with a pathology foundation model, and aggregating them into a patient-level risk prediction.

Histology slides are divided into patches, encoded with a pathology foundation model, and aggregated into a patient-level prediction.

METHODS

The core technical challenges are learning from censored outcomes, estimating treatment benefit without randomized data, and producing calibrated predictions that generalize across institutions.

Survival analysis

Deep survival models are trained on right-censored time-to-event labels, preserving the full event-time distribution rather than reducing outcomes to binary labels.

Multimodal fusion

Patch embeddings from a pathology foundation model are aggregated via multiple instance learning and fused with structured clinical variables to produce a patient-level risk score.

Causal inference

Individualized treatment benefit is estimated from observational cohorts using deep learning-based adjustment, without relying on randomized trial data. These approaches draw on deep learning methods, such as counterfactual regression networks (CFRNet), as well as more traditional doubly robust methods.

Calibration

Post-hoc recalibration aligns predicted risk scores with observed recurrence rates across institutions, so that a predicted 20% risk means 20% in practice. These methods use beta calibration with resampled patient outcomes from the training cohorts.
